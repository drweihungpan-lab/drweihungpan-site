# ADR-0002: The credit policy for Analysis

- **Status:** superseded by [ADR-0003](0003-a-four-page-calling-card.md) — the
  credit policy is **withdrawn**
- **Date:** 2026-10-31
- **Superseded:** 2026-11-02
- **Supersedes:** the "Authorship and research-ethics policy" in §3 of
  `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-personal-website-design.md`

## Withdrawn, 2026-11-02

This policy is **withdrawn, not merely unpublished**, because the work it
governed is no longer offered.
[ADR-0003](0003-a-four-page-calling-card.md) withdraws publication work
entirely: no page offers publication statistics, reviewer response or help
getting into a journal. A rule about whose name may appear on a published output
has nothing left to govern once nobody can buy a contribution to a publication.

Nothing of it appears on the site. The paragraph and the three decided cases are
gone from the pages in both languages, and no page mentions the policy at all —
mentioning it would invite the enquiries it exists to answer.

It is kept here rather than deleted so that a future reader can see the
withdrawal was a decision. The reasoning below is still the reasoning: if
publication work is ever taken on again, this is the rule to start from, and the
alternative recorded here is still the one to reject. It reads in the present
tense and describes a site that no longer exists.

## Context

Analysis is sold as a contribution to work that gets published. The deliverable
is results, figures and reproducible code that a Researcher puts their name on,
so the question of whose work it is cannot be left to each engagement to settle
quietly.

The approved design answered this with an ICMJE summary: authorship needs all
four criteria, a contributor who misses one is acknowledged, and ghostwriting is
not offered. That is accurate but it is a description of the rules, not a
decision. It leaves the two cases that actually arrive undecided — the graduate
Researcher whose thesis is the thing being assessed, and the supervisor who wants
to commission the analysis for someone else's paper.

A Researcher evaluating this service is deciding whether buying it could endanger
their paper or their standing. They need one sentence they can act on, not a
summary of a guideline they could have read themselves.

## Decision

One rule, published as a single paragraph on the Analysis service page:

> If the owner's name can appear in the output, he can run the Analysis. If the
> work is a graded assessment of the Researcher's own competence, he teaches them
> to run it instead.

The rule governs Analysis only. Engineering and Workshops carry no authorship
question: nobody is credited as an author of a data pipeline or a Workshop.

Three cases are decided in advance, so that no engagement has to negotiate them:

1. **A thesis assembled from published papers is allowed.** The competence was
   already assessed when the papers were reviewed; the thesis assembles work that
   carries the owner's name where he contributed to it.
2. **A supervisor commissioning work for a paper is allowed.** The purchaser is
   not the person being assessed, and the output is a paper that can credit him.
3. **An engagement sold as Coaching does not quietly become Analysis.** Where the
   Researcher's own competence is what is being graded, the engagement is
   Coaching and stays Coaching — the Researcher does the work and leaves able to
   defend their own methods. Scope creep here is the failure mode the rule exists
   to prevent, and the one hardest to notice from inside a friendly engagement.

## Alternative considered and rejected

**Take the work and let credit be settled per engagement** — the approved
design's position: state the ICMJE criteria, agree acknowledgement or
co-authorship project by project against the target journal's policy, and
decline only outright ghostwriting.

It is more permissive and it earns more. Every graduate Researcher with a thesis
deadline and funding is a Client under that policy, and that is a large, willing
segment with a genuine, recurring need.

Rejected because the policy that maximises revenue is the one that puts the
Client at risk. A Researcher who buys an analysis their institution expected them
to perform has committed misconduct whether or not the invoice says so, and the
owner's name on the output is what makes the risk mutual. A rule that can be
checked before the first email is also worth more as proof of judgment than a
correct restatement of a guideline: it is published as a credential, not a
disclaimer.

## Consequences

- The policy **refuses revenue**, deliberately and visibly. A future reader
  looking at the thesis segment will see money being turned away and should read
  this ADR before "fixing" it.
- Some of that demand is recovered rather than lost: a Researcher who cannot buy
  Analysis can buy Coaching, which is a smaller sale but a real one, and it is
  the offer that leaves the Researcher able to defend their own methods.
- Qualifying an enquiry needs one question — can the owner's name appear in the
  output? — which is cheap to ask and cheap to answer.
- Coaching needs its own real scope, deliverables and price. It cannot be the
  consolation prize at the bottom of the Analysis page if it is doing this much
  work.
- The three decided cases must stay in sync between this ADR and the page copy in
  both languages. If a fourth case arrives, it is decided here first.
